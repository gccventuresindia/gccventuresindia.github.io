2026-07-20

Structuring Your India Expansion: GCC vs BOT

When global enterprises decide to establish a presence in India, the strategic conversation rarely starts with "if" but rather "how." The two dominant pathways are the Global Capability Center (GCC) and the Business Process Outsourcing (BOT) model. While both leverage India’s talent pool, they serve fundamentally different organizational needs, risk profiles, and long-term objectives.

Choosing incorrectly can lead to operational friction, misaligned incentives, or governance gaps that take years to rectify. This guide clarifies the structural distinctions between these models to help leadership teams make an informed, practical decision.

Defining the Models: Control vs. Transaction

A Global Capability Center (GCC) is an owned, operated, and governed extension of your core enterprise. It is not a vendor; it is a subsidiary or branch office where you hire directly, manage culture directly, and retain intellectual property in-house. The GCC model is designed for strategic transformation, innovation, and deep integration with global HQ functions.

Conversely, a Business Process Outsourcing (BOT) arrangement is a contractual relationship with a third-party provider. You buy outcomes or services, not headcount. The provider manages hiring, infrastructure, and day-to-day supervision. This model is transactional, scalable, and typically focused on specific, well-defined processes such as IT support, back-office operations, or customer service.

The Core Distinction: A GCC is an internal capability you build and own. A BOT is an external service you procure. One is an investment in strategic capacity; the other is a procurement decision for operational efficiency.

Strategic Fit: When to Build vs. When to Buy

The decision between a GCC and a BOT should be driven by the nature of the work and the level of control required. Consider the following practical scenarios:

Choose a GCC When:

  • Intellectual Property is Critical: Your work involves proprietary algorithms, core product development, or sensitive data that must remain strictly within your corporate perimeter.
  • Strategic Alignment is Key: You need the team to operate as an integrated part of your global product or service roadmap, requiring close collaboration with HQ stakeholders.
  • Cultural Integration Matters: You aim to build a distinct internal culture that mirrors your global brand values, rather than adopting a vendor’s established culture.

Choose a BOT When:

  • Focus is on Core Competency: You want to offload non-core, repetitive, or highly standardized processes to free up internal resources for strategic initiatives.
  • Speed to Market is Urgent: A BOT provider has existing infrastructure, trained talent, and compliance frameworks ready to deploy immediately, whereas a GCC requires months of setup.
  • Cost Variability is Preferred: You seek a variable cost structure tied to service levels or volume, rather than the fixed overhead of payroll, benefits, and real estate associated with an owned center.

Risk and Governance Considerations

Each model carries distinct risk profiles that must be weighed during the planning phase. A GCC places the burden of regulatory compliance, employment law, and infrastructure management on your organization. You are directly liable for data security breaches, talent retention issues, and operational failures. However, this direct ownership allows for tighter control over quality standards and data privacy protocols.

A BOT model shifts much of the operational risk to the vendor. The provider is contractually obligated to meet Service Level Agreements (SLAs) and manage workforce fluctuations. However, this introduces a layer of separation between you and your end-users or data. Governance requires rigorous vendor management, clear SLA definitions, and regular performance audits to ensure the provider’s practices align with your enterprise standards.

Conclusion: Aligning Structure with Strategy

There is no universally superior model. The right choice depends on whether you are building a strategic asset or procuring an operational service. Many mature organizations eventually adopt a hybrid approach, starting with BOT for quick wins and transitioning to GCCs for core strategic capabilities as they scale.

Establishing the correct structure requires more than just legal setup; it demands a clear understanding of your long-term operational goals.
