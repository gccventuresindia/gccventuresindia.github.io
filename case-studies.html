Case Study 01 — GCC Setup from Scratch

A mid-size US technology firm building its first India centre

Bengaluru, India · 40+ engineers · 3 months to operations

Challenge
The company needed to stand up an engineering hub in India from zero — no office, no entity, no hires, no local process. They had been burned before: a vendor promised quick setup but delivered compliance missteps and a 10-month delay. The real blocker was never cost. It was governance, talent pipeline speed, and the right operating model for their size.
Approach
We started with a 90-day roadmap, not a 12-month proposal. Week 1: entity structuring (wholly-owned subsidiary vs. rep office — we picked the subsidiary for control and flexibility). Weeks 2–4: office shortlisting (Bengaluru vs. Hyderabad), vendor selection, and a candidate pipeline. Weeks 5–8: first 12 hires (a mix of leads and senior ICs). Weeks 9–12: onboarding, toolchain setup, and first delivery sprint. Throughout, we sat in weekly working sessions with the US leadership — not slide decks.
Results
The centre was operational and delivering within 3 months instead of the typical 6–9. The team grew to 40+ engineers within 12 months. The company saved approximately 35% on fully-loaded engineering costs compared to US hires, while maintaining quality and reducing time-to-market for key features by 20%.

Case Study 02 — Operations Transformation

Finance and shared services transformation for a UK-based enterprise

UK to India · Finance & Shared Services · 30% cost reduction

Challenge
A mid-market UK enterprise had been running finance and shared services from London with a small offshore team. Processes were manual, reporting was fragmented, and the team was stretched thin. Headcount growth was capped, but the volume of work was increasing. They needed a model that could scale without proportional cost increases, while improving accuracy and reporting capability.
Approach
We mapped their end-to-end finance and shared services processes — accounts payable, month-end close, reporting, HR operations, IT service desk — and built a transformation roadmap. The key decision was not just where to move work, but how to restructure the operating model. We designed a hybrid model: higher-touch, judgment-heavy work stayed close to the business; transactional work moved to India with new automation. We also implemented a shared services governance framework with clear SLAs, escalation paths, and KPIs.
Results
Achieved 30% overall cost reduction while improving process accuracy and report turnaround times. The new operating model supported a 40% increase in transaction volume without adding headcount. The company was able to redirect finance resources toward strategic analysis and business partnering — moving the function from a cost centre to a value centre.

Case Study 03 — Executive Coaching

Transforming C-suite capability for a global operations leader

Multi-continental · C-suite transformation · 18-month engagement

Challenge
A senior VP of Global Operations had been promoted to lead a multi-billion dollar business unit spanning 4 countries. The promotion was well-deserved but the transition was not smooth. The leader was managing across time zones, dealing with cultural friction between headquarters and regional teams, and struggling to delegate while maintaining control. The executive coach worked with the leader to reframe the role: from "doer" to "enabler", from "control" to "governance", from "managing people" to "managing capability".
Approach
We ran a structured 18-month engagement with bi-weekly coaching sessions, 360-degree feedback at the start, and monthly check-ins with the reporting manager. The coach worked on three fronts: personal capability (decision-making frameworks, delegation, communication), team dynamics (building a senior leadership team that could operate without constant intervention), and organizational design (aligning the operating model to the new strategy). The coach was not a therapist — they were a practitioner who had been in similar seats.
Results
Within 6 months, the leader had built a functioning senior team that operated independently. Attrition in the direct reports dropped significantly. The business unit achieved its annual targets for the first time in 3 years. The leader reported a 50% reduction in time spent on operational firefighting and significantly more capacity for strategic work. The transformation was sustained — the leader continued to perform at a high level after the coaching engagement concluded.

Case Study 04 — Japan-India GCC Corridor

Bilingual leadership for a Japanese manufacturer expanding into India

Japan to India · Bilingual leadership · 8 months to operations

Challenge
A Japanese manufacturing company was planning to set up a GCC in India but was struggling with cultural misalignment. Previous attempts had failed because the Japanese leadership expected a certain level of formality and process that the Indian team found unnecessary and bureaucratic. The Indian hires were leaving because the communication gap was too wide. They needed someone who could bridge both cultures and build a team that worked authentically, not one that was trying to mimic the other side.
Approach
We brought in a bilingual leader with deep experience in both Japanese and Indian business cultures. The approach was not about translation — it was about building a shared operating language that respected both contexts. We worked with the Japanese leadership to understand what was non-negotiable (quality standards, safety protocols) versus what could be adapted (communication style, decision speed, feedback mechanisms). For the Indian team, we built a culture of clarity and accountability without the cultural baggage.
Results
Within 8 months, the centre was operational with a bilingual leadership team. The Japanese parent company reported a significant improvement in quality metrics and communication effectiveness. Employee retention improved to industry-leading levels, which is unusual for the India GCC space. The company established a model that other Japanese firms in the region subsequently adopted.
