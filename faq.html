QUESTIONS

The things people ask before they get in touch.

Short answers to the questions we hear most. If yours isn't here, ask it directly — we read every enquiry personally.

What does GCC Ventures India actually do? +

We build and scale Global Capability Centres in India, transform GCC, GBS and global operations, and work with founders building ventures that need operating discipline and value creation. In practice that means the mandate, the governance, the talent and the sequencing — not another strategy deck.

When is the right time to build a GCC? +

When the cost of coordinating across time zones starts to exceed the cost of proximity. It usually shows up as decision latency rather than cost — product, pricing and escalation calls that used to take a week now take a fortnight. Building earlier is considerably cheaper than building late.

We already have a GCC. Is there still a case for working together? +

Frequently. An existing centre is usually constrained by mandate, not capability — narrow scope, no decision rights, no route into the global operating review. That is an operating model problem before it is a talent problem, and it is generally faster to fix than to rebuild.

What does operations transformation actually involve? +

Four things, in this order: the mandate (what the centre is accountable for), the governance (who decides what), the operating rhythm (how work actually moves), and the talent mix. Change the sequence and none of it holds together.

Do you work with ventures as well as corporates? +

Yes. Most of the venture work is the same discipline at an earlier stage — operating model, decision rights, and being honest about which metrics matter before you have the scale to be measured on them.

Do you work with companies outside India? +

Yes. Most engagements are India-centred, but the decisions are global — parent-company reporting, P&L ownership, and the politics of being a centre of excellence inside a multinational.

Are you a consultancy, or do you implement? +

Both. We are comfortable with a strategy-only mandate, though most of the value shows up in implementation — an operating rhythm only holds if somebody is accountable for keeping it honest.

How does an engagement usually start? +

With a conversation about the opportunity, not a proposal process. We work out whether the right next step is building, fixing or strengthening — then agree scope and sequence from there. If we are not the right fit for it, we will say so early.

How do we get in touch? +

Through the contact page. Tell us in a few lines what you are looking to build, transform or grow — and pick the closest fit from the four options so we know where to start.

Still deciding whether this is the right fit?

Describe the opportunity and we will tell you what we would do first — including if that is nothing.
